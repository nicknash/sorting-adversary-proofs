\subsection{The refinement guarantee}\label{sec:refinement-guarantee}

We finish by relating an anchor's child-choice cost to the volume change
it produces. The density guarantee controls its depth, while the chosen
weights make the cost almost proportional to that depth.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-cost}]
The refinement identity gives $g=\log f_x(c)$. If the selected anchor has
depth $m$, the anchor guarantee \eqref{eq:tariff} implies
\[
g\ge-4,\qquad m\le\frac{g+4}{1-1/k}.
\]
Consequently, \eqref{eq:anchor-cost} gives
\[
\ell(c)\le\frac{\gamma}{1-1/k}(g+4)+\log k+1.
\]
For $k\ge64$, the estimate
$-\log(1-1/k)\le1/((k-1)\ln2)\le3/(2k)$ gives
$\gamma/(1-1/k)\le1+3/k$. We may therefore choose
\[
a=1+\frac3k,\qquad b=4a+\log k+1.
\]
Because $g+4\ge0$, increasing its coefficient to $a$ preserves the
inequality, including when $g$ is negative. This proves $\ell(c)\le ag+b$.
The chosen coefficients satisfy $a\ge1$ and
$1\le b\le\log k+6<k+1$, as well as the stated asymptotic bounds.
If the refinement follows unsuccessful comparison certification,
\eqref{eq:failure-gain} gives $g>k$.
\end{proof}
