\subsection{The refinement guarantee}\label{sec:refinement-guarantee}

We can now prove the cost bounds used in Section~\ref{sec:refinement-cost}.
The anchor guarantee relates each child-choice cost to its volume gain
through a linear bound with an additive term of order $\log k$. Large
gains absorb this term: narrow refinements supply them individually, and
path batches supply them on average.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:refinement-cost}]
Write $j=\log k$. The refinement identity gives $g=\log f_x(c)$, and the
anchor guarantee \eqref{eq:tariff} implies
\[
g\ge-4,\qquad m\le\frac{g+4}{1-1/k}.
\]
Since $-\log(1-1/k)\le1/((k-1)\ln2)\le3/(2k)$ for $k\ge64$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-depth-cost}
\frac{\gamma}{1-1/k}
\le\frac{1+3/(2k)}{1-1/k}\le1+\frac3k.
\end{equation}
Thus every refinement satisfies
\[
\ell(m)=\gamma m+j+1
\le\left(1+\frac3k\right)(g+4)+j+1.
\]
Subtracting $\lambda g$ gives the bound used in all three cases:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charge-difference}
\ell(m)-\lambda g
\le j+5+\frac{12}{k}-\frac{j+7}{k}\,g.
\end{equation}

For a degree refinement, the lower bound $g\ge-4$ gives
\[
\ell(m)-\lambda g
\le j+5+\frac{4(j+10)}{k}\le j+6,
\]
because $4(j+10)\le k$ for $j\ge6$.
For a narrow refinement, $g>k$ by \eqref{eq:narrow-gain}, so
\eqref{eq:charge-difference} is less than $-2+12/k<0$.

For a complete path batch, Lemma~\ref{lem:path-address} and the anchor
guarantee give the average gain
\[
\overline g=\frac1H\sum_{i=1}^H g_i
\ge(1-1/k)(k-2)-4\ge k-7.
\]
Averaging \eqref{eq:charge-difference} over the batch therefore gives
\[
\frac1H\sum_{i=1}^H\bigl(\ell(m_i)-\lambda g_i\bigr)
\le-2+\frac{7j+61}{k}\le0,
\]
since $7j+61\le2k$ for $j\ge6$. This proves the path-batch bound.
\end{proof}
