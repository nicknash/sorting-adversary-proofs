\subsection{Properties of the operations}\label{sec:operation-properties}

We now state the guarantees used in the analysis. The first concerns
normalization and restriction of a single order-restriction DAG. The
others describe the effect of refinement, the choices made by the local
routines, and consistency of the whole tree with the transcript.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical intervals and coordinate restriction]\label{lem:interior}
Normalization preserves $K(D)$. This region has positive volume exactly
when all the resulting intervals have positive width. In that case the
intervals are canonical. For any $c\in(\ell_x,r_x)$, the feasible region
of \textnormal{\texttt{Restrict}}$(D,x,c)$ is exactly the section of
$K(D)$ with $X_x=c$, measured in the remaining coordinates, and has
positive volume.
\end{lemma}
Appendix~\ref{app:geometry} proves the lemma.

To measure the effect of restriction, suppose $V(D)>0$ and choose $X$
uniformly from $K(D)$. The density $f_x(c)$ of coordinate $X_x$ is the
volume of the section at $c$ divided by $V(D)$. Thus the lemma gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restriction-volume}
V(\operatorname{Restrict}(D,x,c))=V(D)f_x(c).
\end{equation}
This defines the density at every interior $c$ and gives
$\Prb(a<X_x<b)=\int_a^b f_x(t)\,dt$.

\paragraph{The effect of refinement.}
Write $K_v=K(D_v)$ and $V_v=V(D_v)$. To measure the effect of a
refinement on the whole state, take the product of the node volumes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
Z=\prod_v V_v.
\end{equation}
Each item has a position variable at exactly one node, so the product
region has dimension $n$. A node with no free items contributes volume
one. Initially $Z=1$; whenever every node has positive volume,
$0<Z\le1$.

Refinement fixes an item's position in its parent and gives it a fresh
unit coordinate in a child. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refinement-gain}
g=\log\frac{Z_{\rm after}}{Z_{\rm before}}
\end{equation}
for the resulting change in $\log Z$. We call $g$ the refinement's
\emph{volume gain}; it can be negative.

\label{sec:refinement-volume}

The restriction identity \eqref{eq:restriction-volume} now gives the
change in this product. Here $f_x$ is the coordinate density in $D_v$
before the move.

\begin{lemma}[Refinement identity]\label{lem:section-volume}
Suppose every node region has positive volume. Refining $x$ at an interior
anchor $c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refine}
\frac{Z_{\rm after}}{Z_{\rm before}}=f_x(c).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
Appendix~\ref{app:execution} proves the identity.

Thus the refinement's volume gain is $g=\log f_x(c)$.

\paragraph{Choosing answers and anchors.}
The next lemma makes the guarantee of \texttt{Select} precise and
specifies the anchors returned by both local routines. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-threshold}
\eta=\frac1{100k}.
\end{equation}
The temporary bounds $k$ and $2H$ allow for the arc inserted by a comparison
before \texttt{Repair} runs. The final inequality ensures that anchors
requiring many binary digits produce a correspondingly large volume gain.

\begin{lemma}[Local selection]\label{lem:local-routines}
Let $D$ be an order-restriction DAG with canonical dyadic intervals,
$V(D)>0$, degree at most $k$, and directed paths of at most $2H$ vertices.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a comparison $X_a<X_b$ with $a\ne b$, or between $X_a$ and
a dyadic constant, \textnormal{\texttt{Select}} returns either an answer
retaining at least $1/2-\eta$ of $V(D)$ or a queried coordinate $x$
and a dyadic $c\in(\ell_x,r_x)$ with $\log f_x(c)>k$.
\item Given a coordinate $x$, \textnormal{\texttt{ChooseAnchor}}$(D,x)$
returns a dyadic $c\in(\ell_x,r_x)$.
\end{enumerate}
Every returned position $c=(2i+1)/2^{m+1}$ in reduced form satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tariff}
\log f_x(c)\ge(1-1/k)m-4.
\end{equation}
The routines are deterministic and use only the graph and interval bounds
in a neighborhood of the queried coordinates whose radius depends on $k$.
\end{lemma}

Appendix~\ref{sec:oracle} constructs the local calculations, and
Appendix~\ref{app:execution} proves the lemma. Thus \texttt{Select}
either answers while losing at most about one bit of $\log Z$, or
requests a refinement that gains more than $k$ bits. Every repair
anchor also satisfies \eqref{eq:tariff}.
Section~\ref{sec:runtime} states the running-time guarantee.

\paragraph{Consistency and termination.}
The following proposition ensures that these local choices fit together
into a legal adversary. Its proof uses the fact that an item can enter
an existing child only when the transcript leaves its order relative
to the items there unrestricted.

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and termination]\label{prop:legality}
Throughout the recursive phase the state has positive feasible volume,
and every represented order satisfies the transcript. Each call
terminates with at most $n+2$ refinements. The strict degree and path
bounds hold between calls.
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:execution} gives the induction over the state updates.

\paragraph{The comparison guarantee.}
Let $T_q$ be the number of total orders consistent with the first $q$
answers. The preceding guarantees lead to the following bound.

\begin{theorem}[Comparison prefixes]\label{thm:fixed-stage}
For every integer $k\ge64$, the adversary with parameter $k$ is
deterministic and legal. There is a nonnegative
$\delta_k=O(\log k/k)$, independent of $n$ and the query sequence,
such that after any $q$ answers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-orders}
\log T_q\ge\log(n!)-(1+\delta_k)q.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{sec:potential} proves the theorem by first showing that
only $O(q/k)$ refinements occur in $q$ comparisons. When a correct sorter
stops, $T_q=1$, so the theorem forces at least
$\log(n!)/(1+\delta_k)$ comparisons. Section~\ref{sec:runtime} chooses
$k$ as a function of $n$ to obtain the optimal asymptotic bound in
polynomial time.
